# General practice notes

These notes are a starting library, not a substitute for the bare act or the current court rules. Court specific practice directions are not included unless the administrator adds them.

## Cheque dishonour, Negotiable Instruments Act section 138 steps
A cheque must be presented within its validity period of three months from its date. After dishonour the payee must send a written demand notice within 30 days of receiving the bank's information of dishonour. The drawer has 15 days from receipt of the notice to pay. If the drawer does not pay, the complaint must be filed within one month from the day the 15 days expire, under section 142, and the court may condone delay for sufficient cause. Keywords: 138 NI Act, cheque bounce, demand notice, dishonour.

## Cheque dishonour punishment and compensation
Section 138 provides imprisonment up to two years, or fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both. Section 143A allows interim compensation of up to 20 percent of the cheque amount. Section 148 requires an appellant against conviction to deposit at least 20 percent of the fine or compensation. Section 147 makes the offence compoundable. Keywords: 138 NI Act, interim compensation, 143A, 148.

## Cheque dishonour territorial jurisdiction
Under section 142(2) as amended in 2015 the complaint is filed in the court within whose local jurisdiction the branch of the bank where the payee maintains the account is situated, when the cheque is delivered for collection through an account. Keywords: 138 NI Act jurisdiction, 142(2).

## Limitation Act 1963 general periods
The residuary period for suits is three years from when the right to sue accrues. A suit for specific performance has three years from the date fixed for performance, or from when the plaintiff has notice that performance is refused. A suit for possession based on title has twelve years from when the defendant's possession becomes adverse, and a suit for possession based on earlier possession has twelve years from dispossession. Applications for which no period is provided have three years. Keywords: limitation, suit period, possession, specific performance.

## Limitation Act exclusion and extension of time
Section 5 allows delay in filing an appeal or application to be condoned for sufficient cause, but not delay in filing a suit. Section 12 excludes the time spent obtaining a certified copy of the judgment or order. Section 14 excludes time spent bona fide prosecuting a case in a court without jurisdiction. Section 17 postpones the start of limitation in case of fraud or mistake. Sections 18 and 19 give a fresh period on written acknowledgment of liability or part payment. Keywords: condonation of delay, section 5, section 14, limitation exclusion.

## Limitation for civil appeals
An appeal to the High Court from a decree or order of a subordinate court has 90 days. An appeal to a court other than the High Court, for example to the District Judge, has 30 days. The days are counted from the date of the decree or order, excluding time for obtaining copies. Keywords: first appeal, appeal limitation, 90 days, 30 days.

## Limitation for taking cognizance of offences
Under BNSS section 514, earlier CrPC section 468, the court cannot take cognizance after six months if the offence is punishable with fine only, after one year if punishable with imprisonment up to one year, and after three years if punishable with imprisonment above one year and up to three years. There is no bar for offences punishable with imprisonment above three years. Keywords: cognizance limitation, 468 CrPC, 514 BNSS.

## Bail provisions under the BNSS
Bail in non-bailable offences by a Magistrate or court is under BNSS section 480, earlier CrPC 437. The High Court and Court of Session have special powers under section 483, earlier 439. Anticipatory bail is under section 482, earlier 438. An undertrial who has served half of the maximum sentence can be released on personal bond under section 479, earlier 436A, and a first-time offender who has served one third; this does not apply to offences punishable with death or life imprisonment. Keywords: bail, anticipatory bail, undertrial, 479 BNSS.

## Default bail
Under BNSS section 187(3), earlier CrPC 167(2), the accused is entitled to default bail if the investigation is not completed within 90 days for offences punishable with death, life imprisonment or imprisonment of ten years or more, and within 60 days for other offences, provided the accused is ready to furnish bail. Keywords: default bail, statutory bail, 167(2), 187(3), chargesheet not filed.

## Consumer Protection Act 2019 pecuniary jurisdiction
Under the 2021 jurisdiction rules the District Commission hears complaints where the value of goods or services paid as consideration is up to Rs. 50 lakh, the State Commission above Rs. 50 lakh and up to Rs. 2 crore, and the National Commission above Rs. 2 crore. Keywords: consumer court jurisdiction, district commission, state commission, national commission.

## Consumer Protection Act 2019 limitation and appeals
A complaint must be filed within two years from the date the cause of action arises under section 69, with condonation possible for sufficient cause. An appeal from the District Commission to the State Commission is within 45 days, and under section 41 the appellant must deposit 50 percent of the amount ordered. An appeal from the State Commission to the National Commission is within 30 days, and from the National Commission to the Supreme Court within 30 days. Keywords: consumer appeal, section 69, section 41.

## Entering the new criminal codes
The BNS, BNSS and BSA apply to offences committed on or after 1 July 2024. Offences committed earlier continue under the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act, while the procedure for proceedings started after that date follows the BNSS. Keywords: new criminal laws, transition, 1 July 2024.
